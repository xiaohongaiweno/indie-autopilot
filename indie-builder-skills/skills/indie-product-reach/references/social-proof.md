# Social proof system playbook

## Why social proof matters for indie products

Independent products face a trust deficit. Users hesitate: "Is this legit? Will it still be here in 6 months? Can I trust this person with my data?" Social proof answers these objections. It's not about vanity metrics — it's about reducing friction in the decision to try your product.

## Types of social proof

### 1. User testimonials

**What makes a good testimonial**:
- **Specific**: "This saved me 5 hours per week" > "This is great!"
- **Real**: Full name, photo, company/role (with permission)
- **Relatable**: From someone your target user can identify with
- **Outcome-focused**: What problem did it solve? What result did they get?

**How to collect testimonials**:

**Ask at the right moment**:
- After they've achieved value (completed onboarding, hit a milestone, used a key feature)
- After a positive support interaction
- After they've been using the product for 2–4 weeks (not day 1, not year 2)

**Make it easy**:
- Provide a simple template: "What problem were you trying to solve? How did [Product] help? What result did you get?"
- Offer to write a draft they can edit
- Keep it short — 2–4 sentences is ideal

**Ask directly**:
- Email active users: "Would you be willing to share a quick testimonial?"
- Offer to feature them (with permission) on your website
- Don't incentivize with discounts or freebies — it biases the testimonial

**Legal note**: Always get explicit permission to use someone's testimonial. Get it in writing (email is fine). Let them know where it will be used.

**Where to display**:
- Homepage (above the fold if you have strong ones)
- Pricing page (reduces purchase anxiety)
- Landing pages (near the CTA)
- Dedicated testimonials page
- Product Hunt listing
- Directory submissions

### 2. Review platforms

**When to use**:
- Your product is in a category where users check review sites before buying (SaaS, business tools, etc.)
- You have enough users that some will leave reviews organically
- You're B2B or prosumer (less relevant for casual consumer tools)

**Platforms to consider**:

**G2**:
- Major B2B software review platform
- Free to create a listing
- Influential for enterprise and mid-market buyers
- Requires active review collection

**Capterra**:
- Similar to G2, strong in SMB
- Free listing
- Good for project management, productivity, dev tools

**TrustRadius**:
- More enterprise-focused
- In-depth reviews
- Good for higher-priced products

**Product Hunt**:
- Not a traditional review site, but upvotes and comments serve as social proof
- Important for tech-savvy early adopters

**AlternativeTo**:
- Users looking for alternatives to established tools
- Good for comparison-driven decisions

**Niche review sites**:
- Developer tools: StackShare, Dev Hunt
- Design tools: Dribbble, Behance (if applicable)
- Specific categories may have their own review platforms

**How to collect reviews**:
- Email active users with a direct link to the review page
- Ask at the right moment (after value, not on day 1)
- Make it clear you're asking for an honest review, not just a positive one
- Don't incentivize (most platforms prohibit it)
- Respond to reviews (positive and negative) professionally

### 3. GitHub stars and open-source presence

**Why it matters**:
- GitHub stars are a trust signal for developer tools
- Open-source projects get more credibility
- Active repos show you're maintaining the product

**How to build**:
- Open-source part of your stack (a library, a utility, a component)
- Make the README excellent (see developer-channels.md)
- Share on Hacker News, Dev.to, Reddit when you open-source something
- Engage with contributors and issues
- Don't ask for stars directly — it's frowned upon

**Display**:
- GitHub badge on your website: "★ X on GitHub"
- Link to the repo from your product page
- Mention in directory listings

### 4. User showcase / case studies

**What makes a good case study**:
- **Real user**: Named company or individual (with permission)
- **Specific problem**: What were they struggling with?
- **Clear solution**: How did they use your product?
- **Measurable result**: Time saved, revenue gained, errors reduced, etc.
- **Visual**: Screenshots, diagrams, before/after if possible

**How to create**:
- Identify power users who've achieved notable results
- Interview them (15–30 minutes): problem, solution, result, advice
- Write it up in a standard format
- Get their approval before publishing
- Offer to promote it to your audience (they'll share it too)

**Format**:
- **Title**: "How [Company/User] achieved [result] with [Product]"
- **Problem**: 2–3 paragraphs
- **Solution**: 2–3 paragraphs with screenshots
- **Result**: Specific metrics or outcomes
- **Advice**: Quote from the user
- **CTA**: "Try [Product] yourself"

**Where to use**:
- Dedicated case studies page
- Blog posts
- Landing pages
- Sales conversations (if B2B)
- Directory listings

### 5. Usage metrics (with caution)

**What you can show**:
- "X users" or "X downloads" (if impressive)
- "X hours saved" (if you can calculate it)
- "X projects created" or similar activity metrics
- "Trusted by teams at [Company A], [Company B]" (with permission)

**When to show**:
- Only if the numbers are genuinely impressive
- "10 users" is not social proof — it's the opposite
- "10,000 users" is strong social proof
- If you're small, focus on testimonials and case studies instead

**What not to show**:
- Fabricated or inflated numbers
- Metrics that don't mean anything ("X lines of code written")
- Numbers that highlight how small you are

### 6. Media mentions and awards

**What counts**:
- Tech blog coverage (TechCrunch, Hacker News, niche blogs)
- Podcast appearances
- Awards or recognitions (Product Hunt awards, industry awards)
- Conference talks or sponsorships

**How to collect**:
- Pitch your product to tech blogs (see launch-orchestration.md)
- Apply for relevant awards
- Speak at conferences or podcasts
- Share build stories on social media (journalists sometimes pick these up)

**Where to display**:
- "As seen in" section on homepage
- Media kit page
- Directory listings
- Email signatures

## Building a social proof system

### Phase 1: Foundation (month 1–2)
- Set up a testimonials collection process
- Create a landing page or section for testimonials
- List your product on 2–3 relevant review platforms
- Start collecting user stories

### Phase 2: Growth (month 3–6)
- Publish 2–3 case studies
- Actively collect reviews on G2/Capterra/AlternativeTo
- Display testimonials prominently on homepage and pricing page
- Share user stories on social media

### Phase 3: Scale (month 6+)
- Maintain a steady flow of new testimonials and case studies
- Respond to all reviews (positive and negative)
- Use social proof in directory listings and marketing materials
- Track which social proof converts best

## Common mistakes

- **Fabricating testimonials**: Never do this. It's unethical and often illegal.
- **Using vague testimonials**: "Great product!" is worthless. Get specific.
- **Hiding negative reviews**: Respond professionally, don't delete. It looks suspicious.
- **Asking too early**: Don't ask for a testimonial before the user has achieved value.
- **Incentivizing reviews**: Most platforms prohibit it, and it biases the feedback.
- **Showing weak metrics**: "10 users" hurts more than it helps.
- **Forgetting to ask**: Users won't volunteer testimonials. You have to ask.

## Measurement

Track:
- Number of testimonials collected
- Review platform ratings and review counts
- Case studies published
- Conversion rate changes after adding social proof
- Referral traffic from review platforms
