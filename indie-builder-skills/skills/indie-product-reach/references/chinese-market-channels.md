# Chinese market channels playbook

## Why consider the Chinese market

If your product has international potential, the Chinese market offers a large, distinct audience with its own platforms, communities, and marketing channels. The Chinese internet ecosystem is completely separate from the global internet (due to the Great Firewall), so Western platforms like Google, Twitter, Reddit, and YouTube are not accessible. You need platform-specific strategies.

## Key differences from global marketing

- **Different platforms**: WeChat, Zhihu, Bilibili, Xiaohongshu instead of Twitter, Reddit, YouTube
- **Different search engines**: Baidu, Bing China instead of Google
- **Different developer communities**: V2EX, Juejin, CSDN instead of Dev.to, Medium
- **Content regulations**: All content must comply with Chinese internet regulations
- **Language**: Simplified Chinese (简体中文), not Traditional Chinese
- **Payment**: WeChat Pay, Alipay instead of Stripe/PayPal (if you're selling)

## Developer communities

### V2EX (v2ex.com)

**What it is**:
- Chinese-language tech community, similar to Hacker News but more general
- Active developer, entrepreneur, and indie hacker community
- Good for product launches, technical discussions, and feedback

**How to use**:
- Create an account and participate genuinely before promoting
- Post in the "创意" (Ideas) or "分享创造" (Share Creations) section
- Write in Chinese — English posts get less engagement
- Be prepared for direct, sometimes harsh feedback
- Disclose that you're the maker

**What works**:
- Open-source projects
- Developer tools
- Indie product launches
- Technical deep-dives

**What doesn't work**:
- Obvious marketing language
- Products with no clear technical value
- Ignoring feedback or criticism

**Measurement**: Track post URL, date, replies, upvotes, referral traffic, signups

### Juejin (juejin.cn)

**What it is**:
- Chinese developer content platform, similar to Dev.to + Medium
- Focus on tutorials, technical articles, and build logs
- Good for long-tail SEO within the Chinese ecosystem

**How to use**:
- Write technical articles in Chinese
- Use your product as part of the solution (don't make it the focus)
- Tag appropriately: #前端, #后端, #开源, #工具, etc.
- Cross-post from your blog (use canonical URLs)

**Content ideas**:
- "How I built [product]" (我是如何开发[产品]的)
- Technical tutorials that use your product
- Open-source project announcements
- Lessons learned from launching

**Measurement**: Track article URL, date, views, likes, comments, link clicks

### CSDN (csdn.net)

**What it is**:
- Largest Chinese developer community (like Stack Overflow + Medium)
- Huge traffic, but quality varies
- Good for SEO if you can rank

**How to use**:
- Write technical articles and tutorials
- Answer questions related to your product's domain
- Be cautious — CSDN has a reputation for low-quality content, so stand out with quality

**Note**: CSDN is less respected than V2EX or Juejin among serious developers. Use it for reach, not for credibility.

## Content platforms

### Zhihu (zhihu.com)

**What it is**:
- Chinese Quora — Q&A platform with long-form content
- High-quality content, good for building authority
- Good for SEO (Zhihu answers rank well on Baidu)

**How to use**:
- Answer questions related to your product's domain
- Write Zhihu articles (专栏) with in-depth content
- Don't be overly promotional — provide genuine value first
- Disclose affiliation when mentioning your product

**Content ideas**:
- Answer "How do I [problem]?" questions
- Write "What is it like to use [product]?" (使用[产品]是怎样的体验？)
- Share build stories and lessons learned

**Measurement**: Track answer/article URL, date, upvotes, comments, followers gained, link clicks

### WeChat Official Account (微信公众号)

**What it is**:
- WeChat's content platform — like a blog + newsletter
- Massive reach (WeChat has 1.3B users)
- Requires consistent content to build an audience
- Content is walled-garden (not indexed by search engines)

**How to use**:
- Set up a WeChat Official Account (requires Chinese business entity or individual with Chinese ID — see "Challenges" below)
- Publish articles regularly (2–4 per month minimum)
- Share across other platforms (Zhihu, V2EX) with links to your WeChat article
- Build an email-like subscriber base within WeChat

**Challenges**:
- Requires Chinese business entity or individual with Chinese ID to set up
- Content must comply with Chinese regulations
- Building an audience takes time and consistent effort
- Analytics are limited compared to web platforms

**When to use**:
- You're serious about the Chinese market long-term
- You have a way to set up an Official Account
- You're committed to publishing Chinese content regularly

### Bilibili (bilibili.com)

**What it is**:
- Chinese YouTube — video platform, especially popular with younger audiences
- Strong developer/tech community
- Good for tutorials, demos, and build stories

**How to use**:
- Create short, engaging videos (5–15 minutes)
- Add Chinese subtitles (essential — many users watch without sound)
- Upload directly to Bilibili (don't just link to YouTube)
- Engage with comments (弹幕 / danmaku and regular comments)

**Content ideas**:
- Product demos and tutorials
- Build logs and behind-the-scenes
- Technical deep-dives
- "Day in the life" of an indie developer

**Measurement**: Track video URL, date, views, likes, coins (Bilibili's tipping system), comments, followers

### Xiaohongshu (小红书)

**What it is**:
- Lifestyle and product review platform (like Instagram + Pinterest)
- Growing tech/productivity content
- Good for consumer-facing products, especially if visual

**How to use**:
- Share product screenshots, use cases, and reviews
- Write in a casual, personal tone
- Use relevant hashtags
- Disclose affiliation

**When to use**:
- Your product is visual or lifestyle-oriented
- You're targeting younger, urban Chinese users
- You have good visual content (screenshots, photos, short videos)

## Search engine optimization (Baidu SEO)

### Key differences from Google SEO

- **Baidu is the dominant search engine** (though Bing China is growing)
- **Baidu favors Chinese-language content** hosted on Chinese domains or CDNs
- **Baidu's algorithm** is less transparent than Google's
- **Content regulations**: All content must comply with Chinese internet regulations
- **ICP filing**: Websites hosted in mainland China require an ICP license (see below)

### Baidu SEO checklist

- **Register with Baidu Webmaster Tools** (百度站长平台)
- **Submit your sitemap** to Baidu
- **Optimize for Baidu's algorithm**:
  - Chinese-language content (Simplified Chinese)
  - Fast loading in China (use a Chinese CDN if possible)
  - Mobile-friendly (Baidu is heavily mobile)
  - Clear site structure and navigation
- **Build backlinks from Chinese sites**: Baidu trusts Chinese domains (.cn, .com.cn) more
- **Create content on Chinese platforms**: Zhihu, Juejin, CSDN — these rank well on Baidu

### ICP filing (ICP备案)

**What it is**:
- Required for websites hosted on servers in mainland China
- Issued by the Chinese government (MIIT)
- Requires a Chinese business entity or individual with Chinese ID

**When you need it**:
- Hosting your website on servers in mainland China (Alibaba Cloud China, Tencent Cloud, etc.)
- Using a Chinese CDN (some CDNs require ICP)

**When you don't need it**:
- Hosting outside mainland China (Hong Kong, overseas)
- Most indie products can start without ICP by hosting overseas

**How to get it** (if needed):
- Requires Chinese business entity or individual with Chinese ID
- Process takes 1–3 weeks
- Done through your hosting provider (Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, etc.)

## Product directories and launch platforms

### Chinese Product Hunt equivalents

**少数族 (sspai.com)**:
- Curated app/product review site
- High-quality, editorial content
- Pitch your product to their editors

**Product Hunt China / 36Kr (36kr.com)**:
- Major tech media platform
- Covers startups and products
- Pitch via their submission form or contact editors

**最美应用 (zuimeia.com)**:
- Curated app recommendations
- Good for consumer-facing apps

**AppSo (ifanr.com/app)**:
- App recommendations and reviews
- Part of ifanr (爱范儿) tech media

### Developer-specific directories

**GitHub Mirror (gitee.com)**:
- Chinese GitHub alternative
- Host your open-source project here for better access in China
- Good for SEO within China

**OSChina (oschina.net)**:
- Open-source community and directory
- List your open-source projects

## Localization vs. translation

### What to localize

- **Product interface**: At minimum, localize the core experience
- **Documentation**: Translate key docs, tutorials, FAQs
- **Marketing content**: Don't just translate — adapt for Chinese audience
- **Pricing**: Consider purchasing power parity (PPP) — Chinese users expect lower prices
- **Support**: Offer Chinese-language support (even if it's just email)

### What not to localize (yet)

- **Every feature**: Start with core features, expand based on demand
- **Legal pages**: English is usually fine for privacy policy, terms (unless you're selling)
- **Blog posts**: Translate the most important ones, not everything

### Localization tips

- **Hire a native speaker**: Don't rely on machine translation for product UI
- **Use Simplified Chinese**: Not Traditional Chinese (that's for Hong Kong/Taiwan)
- **Cultural adaptation**: Some metaphors, examples, or references don't translate
- **Test with Chinese users**: Get feedback from real Chinese users before launching

## Challenges and considerations

### Barriers to entry

- **Language barrier**: If you don't speak Chinese, you'll need help
- **Payment**: If you're selling, you need WeChat Pay / Alipay (requires Chinese business entity)
- **Regulations**: Content must comply with Chinese internet regulations
- **Hosting**: For best performance, host in China (requires ICP filing)
- **Support**: Chinese users expect Chinese-language support

### When to prioritize China

- You have a product with clear international potential
- You have access to Chinese market resources (language, connections, business entity)
- You're seeing organic demand from Chinese users
- You're committed to long-term investment in the market

### When to deprioritize China

- You're just starting out (focus on your home market first)
- You don't have Chinese language skills or resources
- Your product is very niche with limited Chinese demand
- You can't support Chinese users properly

## Measurement

Track for each Chinese platform:
- Platform name, URL, date
- Engagement: views, likes, comments, shares
- Referral traffic to your product
- Signups/downloads from Chinese channels
- Quality of feedback and conversations
- Long-term retention of Chinese users
