# Competitive intelligence playbook

## Why competitive intelligence matters

You can't position your product effectively without understanding what competitors are doing. Competitive intelligence isn't about copying — it's about learning what works, finding gaps, and making informed decisions about where to focus your limited resources.

## What to monitor

### 1. Directory listings

**What to track**:
- Which directories are they listed on?
- How is their positioning described?
- What categories are they in?
- Are they on free or paid tiers?
- How many reviews/ratings do they have?

**How to find out**:
- Search for their product name on major directories (Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, G2, Capterra, etc.)
- Check niche directories in your category
- Use site: searches: `site:producthunt.com "[competitor]"`

**What to do with it**:
- If they're on a directory you're not on, evaluate whether it's worth listing
- If their positioning is different from yours, understand why
- If they have many reviews, study what users praise/complain about

### 2. Keyword strategy

**What to track**:
- What keywords are they targeting?
- What pages rank for those keywords?
- What content are they producing (blog posts, tutorials, comparison pages)?
- Are they targeting "alternative" or "vs" keywords?

**How to find out**:

**Free methods**:
- Search for their brand name and see what comes up
- Search for "[their product] alternative" or "[their product] vs"
- Look at their blog and note topics they cover
- Check their sitemap (usually at `/sitemap.xml`) for page structure
- Use Google autocomplete: type "[their product] + [letter]" and see suggestions

**Free tools**:
- **Ubersuggest** (free tier): Limited keyword data, but useful
- **Google Keyword Planner** (free with Google Ads account): Search volume estimates
- **Ahrefs Free Tools**: Limited backlink and keyword checks
- **AnswerThePublic** (free tier): Question-based keyword ideas

**What to do with it**:
- Identify keywords they're targeting that you're not
- Find gaps: keywords they're not targeting that you could
- Learn from their content strategy: what topics get the most traction?
- Don't copy blindly — adapt to your product's strengths

### 3. Backlink sources

**What to track**:
- Where are they getting backlinks from?
- What type of content earns links (tutorials, tools, research)?
- Are there resource pages or directories that link to them?
- Are they doing guest posts or partnerships?

**How to find out**:

**Free methods**:
- **Ahrefs Free Backlink Checker**: Shows top 100 backlinks
- **Moz Link Explorer** (free tier): Limited backlink data
- **Google search**: `link:[their-url]` (incomplete but free)
- Look at their "mentions" or "press" page if they have one

**What to do with it**:
- If a site links to them, they might link to you (if you're relevant)
- Identify link-building opportunities: resource pages, directories, tutorials
- Understand what content earns links in your category
- Don't target spammy or irrelevant sources just because they do

### 4. Content strategy

**What to track**:
- What topics do they blog about?
- How often do they publish?
- What formats work (tutorials, case studies, opinion pieces)?
- What gets shared or commented on?
- Are they doing video, podcasts, or other content types?

**How to find out**:
- Read their blog (subscribe to their newsletter if they have one)
- Check their social media: what content gets engagement?
- Look at their YouTube channel if they have one
- See what they're sharing on social media

**What to do with it**:
- Identify content gaps: topics they're not covering that you could
- Learn from their successes: what topics resonate with your shared audience?
- Don't copy their content — create something better or different
- If they're doing video and you're not, consider whether you should

### 5. Product positioning and messaging

**What to track**:
- How do they describe their product on the homepage?
- What's their primary value proposition?
- Who is their target audience?
- What pricing model do they use?
- What features do they emphasize?

**How to find out**:
- Read their homepage, pricing page, about page
- Sign up for a free trial or demo (if available)
- Read their documentation or onboarding emails
- Check their Product Hunt listing or directory descriptions

**What to do with it**:
- Understand how you're different (or if you're not)
- Identify positioning gaps: are they ignoring a segment you could serve?
- Learn from their messaging: what resonates with users?
- Don't copy — differentiate

### 6. Community and social presence

**What to track**:
- Where are they active (Twitter, Reddit, Discord, etc.)?
- How do they engage with their community?
- What do users say about them (positive and negative)?
- How do they handle criticism or support requests?

**How to find out**:
- Follow them on social media
- Join their community (Discord, Slack, forum) if they have one
- Search for mentions on Reddit, Hacker News, Twitter
- Read reviews and comments on directory listings

**What to do with it**:
- Learn from their community engagement (good and bad)
- Identify unmet needs: what are users complaining about?
- Find channels where they're active that you're not
- Don't poach their users directly — provide genuine value

## Setting up a competitive intelligence workflow

### Monthly competitive review (30–60 minutes)

**Step 1: Directory check** (10 min)
- Search for each competitor on 3–5 major directories
- Note any new listings, reviews, or positioning changes
- Record in a tracking spreadsheet

**Step 2: Content scan** (15 min)
- Read their latest blog posts (skim titles and intros)
- Check their social media for recent posts
- Note any new content formats or topics

**Step 3: Keyword check** (15 min)
- Search for "[competitor] alternative" and see what ranks
- Check if they're targeting new keywords
- Note any new comparison pages or content

**Step 4: Backlink spot-check** (10 min)
- Use Ahrefs free checker to see if they've earned notable new links
- Look for patterns: are they doing guest posts? Getting featured in roundups?

**Step 5: Synthesis** (10 min)
- What did you learn?
- What should you do differently?
- Are there opportunities you're missing?
- Record findings and action items

### Tracking template

Create a simple spreadsheet or document:

| Competitor | Date Checked | New Directories | New Content | Keywords Targeted | Notable Backlinks | Positioning Changes | Action Items |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Tools for competitive intelligence

### Free tools
- **Google Alerts**: Get email notifications when competitors are mentioned
- **Ahrefs Free Tools**: Limited but useful backlink and keyword checks
- **Ubersuggest** (free tier): Keyword and site analysis
- **Google Search**: `site:`, `link:`, and brand name searches
- **Social media**: Follow competitors, set up lists or notifications
- **RSS readers**: Subscribe to competitor blogs

### Paid tools (if budget allows)
- **Ahrefs** ($99+/month): Comprehensive SEO and backlink analysis
- **SEMrush** ($119+/month): Keyword research, competitive analysis
- **Moz Pro** ($99+/month): SEO analytics and competitive insights
- **SimilarWeb** (free tier available): Traffic and audience analysis

### When to invest in paid tools
- You've validated your product and have revenue
- You're spending significant time on SEO and content
- Free tools aren't giving you enough data to make decisions
- You're competing in a crowded category where intelligence matters

## Ethical considerations

### Do:
- Use publicly available information only
- Sign up for free trials like a normal user
- Read their public content and social media
- Analyze their positioning and messaging
- Learn from their successes and failures

### Don't:
- Hack into their systems or accounts
- Use fake identities to gather information
- Copy their content, code, or designs
- Poach their employees or users directly
- Engage in industrial espionage
- Violate terms of service to gather data

## Acting on competitive intelligence

### When to adapt
- They're ranking for keywords you should be targeting
- They're on directories you're not on (and it's relevant)
- Their content strategy is working and you can learn from it
- Users are complaining about something you could solve better

### When to ignore
- They're doing something that doesn't fit your product or audience
- They're pursuing a strategy you can't sustain (e.g., heavy paid ads)
- The opportunity is too small or niche
- It would dilute your positioning

### When to differentiate
- Everyone in your category is doing the same thing
- There's a gap in the market they're ignoring
- You can serve a segment they're not serving
- You can do something fundamentally different (pricing, model, audience)

## Measurement

Track:
- Number of competitors monitored
- Frequency of competitive reviews
- Opportunities identified and acted on
- Keywords where you've closed the gap with competitors
- Directories where you've matched or exceeded competitor presence
- Content gaps filled based on competitive analysis
