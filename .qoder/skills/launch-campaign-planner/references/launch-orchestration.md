# Launch orchestration playbook

## Why launch orchestration matters

A product launch is a concentrated burst of attention. Done well, it creates momentum that carries forward. Done poorly, it's a one-day spike that fizzles. Orchestration means coordinating multiple channels to maximize impact on launch day and sustain it afterward.

## Phase 1: Pre-launch (2–4 weeks before)

### Build anticipation

**Landing page**:
- Clear value proposition
- Email signup for launch notification
- Countdown or "coming soon" messaging
- Share on social media to start building an audience

**Email list**:
- If you have an existing list, tease the launch
- If not, start collecting emails now (see email-list-building.md)
- Offer early-bird pricing or exclusive access for subscribers

**Social media**:
- Start posting build updates (see developer-channels.md for Build in Public)
- Share screenshots, progress, behind-the-scenes
- Build a narrative: "Launch day is coming"

**Beta users**:
- Recruit 10–50 beta users 1–2 weeks before launch
- Get feedback, fix critical issues, collect testimonials
- Beta users can amplify on launch day

### Prepare assets

**Launch copy** (prepare in advance, adapt per platform):

- **Product Hunt**: Tagline, description, first comment, gallery images (see below)
- **Hacker News**: Show HN post draft (see developer-channels.md)
- **Reddit**: Platform-specific posts for relevant subreddits
- **Email**: Launch announcement email
- **Social**: Twitter/X thread, LinkedIn post, etc.
- **Press/blog pitch**: If targeting tech blogs, prepare a pitch document

**Visual assets**:
- Product logo (multiple sizes)
- Screenshots (3–5 key features, annotated if helpful)
- Demo video or GIF (30–60 seconds, show core value)
- Thumbnail for social sharing (1200x630px for Open Graph)

**Technical preparation**:
- Open Graph tags on your website (og:title, og:description, og:image)
- Twitter Card tags
- Analytics tracking for launch traffic
- Server capacity: can your site handle a traffic spike?
- Support plan: who's answering questions/bugs on launch day?

### Coordinate timing

**Pick a launch date**:
- Tuesday–Thursday are generally best
- Avoid major holidays, conferences, or competing launches
- Check Product Hunt calendar to avoid direct competitors

**Create a launch timeline**:
- T-2 weeks: Finalize all copy and assets
- T-1 week: Beta users onboarded, final feedback incorporated
- T-2 days: Schedule social media posts, prepare email
- T-1 day: Final check of all links, forms, analytics
- T-0 (launch day): Execute the plan (see below)

## Phase 2: Launch day

### Hour-by-hour coordination

**Morning (6–9 AM Pacific)**:
- 6:00 AM: Product Hunt goes live at 12:01 AM PT, but early morning is when it gets traction
- 7:00 AM: Post on Hacker News (Show HN)
- 8:00 AM: Post on relevant subreddits (check each subreddit's rules on timing)
- 9:00 AM: Send launch email to your list

**Mid-morning (9–11 AM)**:
- Post on X/Twitter (thread or single post)
- Post on LinkedIn if relevant
- Share in relevant Slack/Discord communities
- Respond to every comment, question, and upvote

**Afternoon (12–5 PM)**:
- Continue engagement on all platforms
- Monitor for bugs or issues — fix critical ones immediately
- Share updates: "Thanks for the support! Just hit X upvotes/users"
- Post follow-up content if appropriate

**Evening (5–9 PM)**:
- Thank supporters publicly
- Share metrics if appropriate: "We hit X signups in Y hours!"
- Plan next day's follow-up

### Platform-specific execution

**Product Hunt**:
- Launch at 12:01 AM PT for maximum visibility
- First comment from the maker: tell the story, why you built it, what problem it solves
- Respond to every comment within hours
- Don't ask for upvotes explicitly — it's against the rules
- Share on social media, but don't spam

**Hacker News**:
- Post Show HN between 7–9 AM PT on a weekday
- Write a thoughtful submission text (see developer-channels.md)
- Respond to every comment, especially technical questions
- Don't be defensive about criticism

**Reddit**:
- Post in 2–3 relevant subreddits (not more, or you look like spam)
- Adapt the post to each subreddit's culture and rules
- Disclose that you're the maker
- Engage with comments genuinely

**Email**:
- Send launch email in the morning (9–11 AM recipient's time zone)
- Subject line: clear and exciting, not clickbait
- Body: what you launched, why it matters, link to product, ask for feedback/sharing

**Social media**:
- Twitter/X thread: tell the story, show screenshots, link to product
- LinkedIn: more professional tone, focus on the problem and solution
- Share in relevant communities (Indie Hackers, Dev.to, etc.)

### Real-time monitoring

- **Analytics**: Watch traffic, signups, conversions in real time
- **Comments**: Respond to every platform within hours
- **Bugs**: Have a plan for critical issues (hotfix, status page, social media update)
- **Sentiment**: Are people excited? Confused? Critical? Adjust messaging if needed

## Phase 3: Post-launch (days 2–14)

### Maintain momentum

**Day 2–3**:
- Share launch results: "We hit X signups in 48 hours!"
- Answer follow-up questions from launch day
- Post user testimonials or feedback (with permission)
- Continue engagement on all platforms

**Week 1**:
- Publish a "launch retrospective" blog post or thread
- Share what worked, what didn't, what you learned
- Follow up with beta users and early adopters
- Start planning the next content push (tutorial, case study, etc.)

**Week 2**:
- Analyze launch data: which channels performed best?
- Double down on what worked
- Start building toward the next milestone (feature launch, user milestone, etc.)
- Keep the narrative going: "Here's what we're building next"

### Sustain long-term

- **Regular updates**: Share progress weekly or biweekly
- **User stories**: Highlight how users are using your product
- **Content**: Continue publishing tutorials, build logs, lessons learned
- **Community**: Build a community around your product (Discord, forum, etc.)
- **Iteration**: Use launch feedback to improve the product

## Launch checklist

### 2 weeks before
- [ ] Landing page live with email signup
- [ ] Beta users recruited and onboarded
- [ ] Launch copy drafted for all platforms
- [ ] Visual assets created (logo, screenshots, video)
- [ ] Open Graph and Twitter Card tags added
- [ ] Analytics tracking configured
- [ ] Server capacity checked
- [ ] Support plan in place

### 1 week before
- [ ] All copy finalized
- [ ] Beta feedback incorporated
- [ ] Launch date and time confirmed
- [ ] Social media posts scheduled or ready
- [ ] Email scheduled or ready to send
- [ ] Final link check (all URLs work)

### Launch day
- [ ] Product Hunt post live
- [ ] Hacker News post live
- [ ] Reddit posts live
- [ ] Email sent
- [ ] Social media posts published
- [ ] Monitoring comments and engagement
- [ ] Bugs monitored and fixed
- [ ] Metrics tracked

### Post-launch
- [ ] Launch results shared
- [ ] Follow-up content published
- [ ] Data analyzed
- [ ] Next steps planned

## Common mistakes

- **Launching on a Friday or weekend**: Lower visibility
- **Not preparing assets in advance**: scrambling on launch day
- **Ignoring comments**: looks like you don't care
- **Asking for upvotes explicitly**: against Product Hunt rules, looks desperate
- **Launching without a support plan**: bugs happen, be ready
- **One-and-done**: launch is the start, not the end
- **Spreading too thin**: better to do 3–4 platforms well than 10 poorly

## Measurement

Track for each platform:
- Launch URL, date, time
- Engagement: upvotes, comments, shares, reactions
- Traffic: referral sessions, peak traffic time
- Conversions: signups, downloads, purchases
- Quality: feedback, testimonials, follow-up opportunities
- Long-term: retention of launch users after 7/30/90 days
