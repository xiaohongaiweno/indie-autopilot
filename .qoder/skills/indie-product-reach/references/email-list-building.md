# Email list building playbook

## Why owned audience matters

Social media algorithms change, platform policies shift, and third-party channels can disappear. An email list is the one channel you own. For indie products, it's the highest-ROI long-term asset: every launch, feature, or content piece gets an instant distribution channel.

## Start from day one

Don't wait until launch. Collect emails from the moment you have a landing page.

### Pre-launch email collection

**Landing page signup**:
- Clear value proposition above the fold
- Single email input field with a CTA like "Get early access" or "Notify me at launch"
- Set expectations: "We'll email you when we launch. No spam."
- Show subscriber count if you have social proof ("Join 200+ developers waiting")

**Lead magnets** (optional but effective):
- Offer something valuable in exchange for email:
  - Early access / beta invite
  - Free template, checklist, or resource related to your product's domain
  - Exclusive content (build logs, tutorials, behind-the-scenes)
  - Launch-day discount
- Don't offer something irrelevant just to get emails

### Post-launch email collection

**In-product signup**:
- After onboarding or first value moment: "Want updates on new features?"
- Settings page: email notification preferences (opt-in by default where legally allowed)
- Don't interrupt core workflow with signup prompts

**Content-driven signup**:
- Blog posts: "Enjoyed this? Subscribe for more [topic] content"
- Tutorials: "Get the full guide + future tutorials via email"
- Resource pages: gated download in exchange for email (only if genuinely valuable)

**Website footer / sidebar**:
- Persistent but non-intrusive signup form
- Clear value: "Product updates and [topic] tips. No spam."

## Email types and cadence

### Launch announcement
- **When**: Launch day (see launch-orchestration.md)
- **Subject**: Clear and exciting, not clickbait
- **Content**: What you launched, why it matters, link to product, ask for feedback
- **Length**: Short — 3–5 paragraphs max

### Product updates
- **When**: Every 2–4 weeks, or when you ship something meaningful
- **Subject**: "[Product] update: [feature name]" or similar
- **Content**: What's new, why it matters, how to use it, screenshots if visual
- **Length**: Scannable — use headers and bullet points

### Content newsletters
- **When**: Weekly or biweekly if you publish regularly
- **Subject**: "[Topic] tips", "This week in [domain]", etc.
- **Content**: Your latest blog posts, tutorials, or curated resources
- **Value**: Don't just promote your product — share genuinely useful content

### Milestone celebrations
- **When**: User milestones, revenue milestones, anniversaries
- **Subject**: "We just hit [X] users!" or "One year of [Product]"
- **Content**: Thank subscribers, share what you learned, what's next
- **Tone**: Personal, authentic, grateful

### Re-engagement
- **When**: Quarterly, for subscribers who haven't opened in 60+ days
- **Subject**: "Still interested in [topic]?" or "We miss you"
- **Content**: Remind them what they signed up for, highlight best recent content, offer an easy out ("Click here to unsubscribe if this isn't relevant anymore")
- **Goal**: Clean your list or re-engage — both are valuable

## Writing effective emails

### Subject lines
- Keep it under 50 characters when possible
- Be specific: "New feature: Dark mode" > "Exciting update!"
- Avoid clickbait: don't promise something the email doesn't deliver
- Test different styles: questions, numbers, personal tone

### Body content
- **Lead with value**: What's in it for the reader?
- **Be concise**: Respect their time. If it can be said in 3 paragraphs, don't use 10.
- **One clear CTA**: What should they do after reading? (Click a link, try a feature, reply with feedback)
- **Personal tone**: You're a person, not a corporation. Write like you talk.
- **Plain text or simple HTML**: Fancy templates often look worse than plain text from a real person

### Legal requirements
- **Unsubscribe link**: Required by law (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, etc.) — make it prominent
- **Physical address**: Required by CAN-SPAM — use your business address or PO box
- **Privacy policy**: Link to it, explain what you do with emails
- **Double opt-in**: Recommended (required in some jurisdictions) — confirm subscription via email
- **Data handling**: Don't share your list with third parties without explicit consent

## Choosing an email platform

### Free or low-cost options for indie products

**Buttondown**:
- Simple, developer-friendly
- Free tier up to 100 subscribers
- Good for newsletters and product updates

**MailerLite**:
- Free tier up to 1,000 subscribers
- Automation, landing pages, forms
- Good all-around choice

**ConvertKit**:
- Free tier up to 1,000 subscribers
- Creator-focused, good for content newsletters
- Powerful automation

**Substack**:
- Free, but takes 10% of paid subscriptions
- Built-in audience discovery
- Good for content-first newsletters, less ideal for product updates

**Self-hosted (Listmonk, etc.)**:
- Full control, no per-subscriber cost
- Requires setup and maintenance
- Good if you're technical and want independence

### What to look for
- Free tier that fits your budget
- Automation (welcome sequence, re-engagement)
- Good deliverability (check reviews)
- Easy migration if you outgrow it
- API access if you want to integrate with your product

## Automation sequences

### Welcome sequence (3–5 emails over 2 weeks)

**Email 1 (immediate)**:
- Welcome, thank them for subscribing
- Deliver the lead magnet if promised
- Set expectations: what they'll receive, how often

**Email 2 (day 2–3)**:
- Introduce yourself / your product
- Tell the story: why you built this, what problem it solves
- Link to your best content or product page

**Email 3 (day 5–7)**:
- Share a tip, tutorial, or use case
- Show value beyond the product itself

**Email 4 (day 10–14)**:
- Ask for feedback or a reply
- "What are you working on?" or "What's your biggest [domain] challenge?"
- Build a relationship, not just a list

### Onboarding sequence (for product users)

**Email 1 (after signup)**:
- Welcome to the product
- One clear next step: "Here's how to get started"
- Link to onboarding docs or video

**Email 2 (day 2)**:
- Highlight a key feature they haven't used yet
- Show a specific use case or tutorial

**Email 3 (day 5)**:
- Check in: "How's it going?"
- Offer help: link to support, docs, or community
- Ask for feedback

**Email 4 (day 10)**:
- Share advanced tips or lesser-known features
- Show what power users do

**Email 5 (day 14)**:
- Ask for a testimonial or review (if they're active)
- Invite them to join your community or follow on social

## Growing your list

### Organic growth tactics
- **Content marketing**: Every blog post, tutorial, or resource is a signup opportunity
- **Social media**: Link to your landing page in bios, mention newsletter in posts
- **Product Hunt / directories**: Link to landing page, not just product
- **Speaking / podcasts**: Mention your newsletter, link in show notes
- **Guest posts**: Include a bio with newsletter link
- **Referral**: "Know someone who'd find this useful? Forward this email"

### What doesn't work
- Buying email lists (illegal in many places, always ineffective)
- Adding people without consent (even if they downloaded something unrelated)
- Forced opt-ins (pre-checked boxes, required for unrelated actions)
- Spamming social media with "subscribe to my newsletter"

## Measurement

Track:
- Total subscribers (and growth rate)
- Open rate (healthy: 40–60% for small lists, 20–40% for larger)
- Click-through rate (healthy: 2–5%)
- Unsubscribe rate (healthy: <0.5% per email)
- Conversion rate (subscriber → user, subscriber → customer)
- Revenue attributed to email (if applicable)

## Quality gates

- Never add someone to your list without explicit consent
- Always include an unsubscribe link
- Don't share your list with third parties
- Don't fabricate subscriber counts or engagement metrics
- Respect unsubscribe requests immediately
- Don't send just to hit a cadence — only send when you have something valuable to say
